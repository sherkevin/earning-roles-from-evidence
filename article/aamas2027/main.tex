\begin{abstract}
Multi-agent systems must assign responsibility before they know which collaborator will make a dependency usable, yet downstream failure entangles producer defects with recipient integration. Existing reputation, acceptance, and terminal-reward schemes collapse these sources: they can rank agents, but cannot tell whether a recipient's concrete use of a delivery is evidence about the producer or about the recipient. We introduce \emph{earning roles}, a responsibility-aware protocol that binds a versioned delivery to the recipient's observable use, filters it through an ownership gate, publishes the resulting evidence before the next assignment, and applies delayed selected-only credit only to that future opportunity. The protocol makes a falsifiable prediction: under matched information, candidate sets, and complete cost, situated evidence should improve the assignment of unseen responsibilities only when it adds producer-attributable information beyond acceptance and terminal outcomes. We formalize the event and read-cut boundaries and specify candidate ArtifactRole and PeerSelect evaluation tracks with same-information baselines, root-level splits, and latency, forgetting, attribution, and cost measures; general expertise, universal reputation, and unbounded workflow discovery remain outside the claimed scope.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

In dependency-bearing multi-agent workflows, a collaborator's value is observed through another agent's attempt to use its artifact. How can collaborators earn future responsibilities from the judgments of agents who use their work? Consider two constructed queue-processing cases with the same rejection and failed team outcome. In one, a producer violates the priority-module contract; in the other, the module is correct but the recipient implements its consumer incorrectly. The same feedback calls for different responsibility assignments. Observing only rejection and terminal failure cannot distinguish the cases; the recipient's account of what it used and why it failed may provide the missing evidence.

This example isolates responsibility confounding: the same feedback can describe producer correctness, recipient integration, or neither. The recipient has direct evidence of the delivery's use, but its judgment can also reflect its own integration difficulties. Learning from that judgment requires separating these sources and testing whether the resulting evidence predicts useful future assignments. A later outcome can inform subsequent decisions; it cannot retrospectively alter the information available to an already sealed assignment.

This paper studies a sharper question: \emph{when can the agent that actually receives and uses a delivery provide reliable evidence about the producer's future responsibility?} The question has a necessary sequence:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
&\text{delivery}\rightarrow\text{recipient use and judgment}\rightarrow\text{producer attribution}\\
&\rightarrow\text{future assignment}\rightarrow\text{unseen quality and cost}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:story}
\end{equation}
A final score cannot by itself identify which producer caused a defect. An acceptance bit cannot distinguish a useful artifact from an easy integration. A recipient's normal integration work cannot automatically become a producer penalty. A role posterior that never changes a later pre-execution assignment is not role formation.

The central idea is to make the recipient's local observation a public, version-bound evidence object with a responsibility boundary. A producer is credited only when the delivery was observed, the recipient action is traceable to that delivery, and a contract check excludes recipient-owned work. Evidence is published without silently updating the policy. Before a later task starts, a legal owner reads the snapshot and the selector seals its assignment and propensity. Only after the later task has an independent outcome can a delayed credit update occur. This separates what a recipient saw from what the future responsibility actually produced, and makes the separation observable in the ledger.

Figure~\ref{fig:overview} summarizes this sequence as one responsibility-evidence loop; the colored paths distinguish situated observation, public evidence, and the later decision.

We name this protocol \emph{earning roles}. The name describes a measurable process: a collaborator earns responsibility opportunities through attributable evidence that changes future assignment under a fixed information and cost contract. We call this responsibility-aware role evidence (RARE); the name refers to the loop's evidence semantics, not to a particular updater. RARE is defined by the judgment--attribution--assignment loop, not by a particular representation, neural head, RLS update, or periodic refit. The scientific claim to test is that this loop identifies useful role evidence beyond raw acceptance, terminal-only outcomes, and same-information contextual trust.

The paper makes four concrete contributions as a testable design.
\begin{itemize}
  \item We define an event and information contract that separates producer quality, recipient action, downstream adoption, final outcome, and \unknown{} while preserving the exact delivery, version, read cut, and propensity.
  \item We give a two-stage role-evidence protocol: publication is immutable and does not change the policy; a later assignment is sealed before execution; delayed selected-only credit can affect only the future opportunity it evaluates. These boundaries make noninterference and idempotent replay testable.
  \item We specify an ArtifactRole track for responsibility diagnosis and a secondary PeerSelect mechanism track, with matched baselines, root-level splits, and complete resource accounting to distinguish information value from closed-loop team benefit.
  \item We state the falsification boundary. If recipient evidence has no out-of-sample information, if a same-information trust baseline explains the effect, if attribution is unsafe, or if quality gains disappear at matched complete cost, the role-formation claim does not pass.
\end{itemize}

The distinction from ordinary online learning is temporal and semantic. A source judgment is available before a later assignment, but the later quality outcome is available only after that assignment has executed. A target outcome may update later decisions once it arrives. Using it to reconstruct an earlier assignment would violate that assignment's information boundary; treating it as a label of the source artifact would also require a separate attribution argument. Our protocol makes the two events explicit and reports the service cost of maintaining the separation. Bandit estimators therefore provide useful update comparators; they are not themselves the claimed contribution.

\section{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:problem}

\subsection{Objects and information boundary}
At episode $t$, the task supplies context $x_t$ and a legal candidate set $C_t$. The selector chooses one candidate before any candidate in $C_t$ executes:
\begin{equation}
 a_t\sim\pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid x_t,C_t,R_t),\qquad a_t\in C_t,
 \label{eq:choice}
\end{equation}
where $R_t$ is the public evidence snapshot available at read cut $w_t$. Only the selected candidate is charged for execution. The candidate emits a versioned delivery $o_t$ with digest $d_t$; a recipient $j_t$ reads that delivery and takes action $m_t$. The recipient may accept, revise, reject, or be unable to complete the integration.

The smallest auditable event is
\begin{equation}
 e_t=(x_t,C_t,a_t,o_t,j_t,J_t,m_t,Q_t,Y_t,\tau_t,c_t),
 \label{eq:event}
\end{equation}
where $J_t$ is the recipient judgment, $Q_t$ is the producer-contract check, $Y_t$ is an independent later or terminal outcome when the task defines one, $\tau_t$ is feedback-arrival metadata, and $c_t$ is the complete cost record. The event also stores the candidate version, task/root identifier, artifact digest, feature-schema version, selection probability $p_t=\pi_\theta(a_t\mid x_t,C_t,R_t)$, read-cut digest, and evidence lineage.

\paragraph{Concrete instance.} In the queue case, $x_t$ is the JSON task request, $C_t=\{A,B\}$ is the legal producer menu, and $a_t=B$ selects producer $B$. The delivery $o_t$ is $B$'s versioned priority-module artifact with digest $d_t$; recipient field $j_t=k$ records $k$'s import, test, or repair action as $m_t$; $Q_t$ checks only the producer-owned queue and priority paths; $Y_t$ is the later consumer outcome; and $c_t$ contains model, tool, token, and wall-clock cost.

A repair to $k$'s consumer path is recorded in $m_t$ and sets the responsibility gate to $G_t=0$ rather than changing $B$'s producer evidence.

The policy may consume only public events whose arrival is at or before its read cut. Hidden gold, a private scorer, a future target outcome, another policy's state, and an unexecuted candidate cannot affect selection, retry, stopping, or update. A missing or non-attributable event is \unknown{}, not a negative label. This boundary is the difference between a role signal and a convenient retrospective score.

\subsection{Responsibility gate}
For a source episode, define $G_t=1$ only when all of the following hold: (i) the producer contract or a pre-registered source defect is uniquely bound to the producer; (ii) the recipient inspected $o_t$ and its action is traceable to $o_t$; (iii) recipient-owned integration paths are empty; (iv) $J_t$, $m_t$, $Q_t$, identifiers, versions, and digests are complete; and (v) the source event has not already been credited. Otherwise $G_t=0$ and the source cannot create producer evidence.

This gate distinguishes four observations that are often collapsed: a producer-content defect, a recipient's own integration work, a sink or final-task failure, and an unresolved attribution. The public record stores the reason for $G_t=0$. This makes safety measurable: a method that refuses ambiguous evidence may have a higher \unknown{} rate while producing fewer false producer penalties.

\section{Earning Roles: A Responsibility-Aware Protocol}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Two-stage evidence and update}
The protocol has two independent interfaces:
\begin{align}
 \operatorname{publish}(e_t)&\rightarrow \{\text{RoleEvidence},\;\unknown{},\;\text{invalid}\},\\
 \operatorname{choose}(x_t,C_t,R_t)&\rightarrow (a_t,p_t,\delta_t),\\
 \operatorname{validate}(j_t)&\rightarrow \{\text{credit},\;\unknown{},\;\text{invalid}\},\\
 \operatorname{update}(\text{credit})&\rightarrow \{\text{updated once},\;\text{no-op}\}.
 \label{eq:interfaces}
\end{align}
Publication may change the public input to a later assignment, but it does not call the persistent updater. For a later target assignment $j$, credit is legal only if the assignment was written before target selection and task start, the selected candidate and propensity match the sealed preview, the target delivery and outcome are complete, and the assignment has not already been credited. The update then evaluates that future responsibility opportunity, never the source episode.

The event-time contract is therefore
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
&\text{source selection}\rightarrow\text{delivery}\rightarrow(Q,J,m,Y)\rightarrow G\\
&\rightarrow\text{evidence publication}\rightarrow\text{assignment}\rightarrow\text{target outcome}\\
&\rightarrow\text{delayed selected-only update}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:timeline}
\end{equation}
A correction creates a superseding evidence version and affects only future snapshots. Duplicate, late, out-of-order, resource-failed, or invalid events are idempotent no-ops or explicit \unknown{} records. Ledger replay must reproduce the same state digest and choice sequence.

\subsection{Evidence representation and candidate score}
A minimal public state for producer $v$ in context $r$ is
\begin{equation}
 R(v,r)=(n(v,r),s(v,r),u(v,r)),
 \label{eq:state}
\end{equation}
where $n$ counts eligible evidence, $s$ accumulates responsibility-safe positive/negative labels, and $u$ records unresolved or rejected evidence for audit. A candidate score can combine a task representation $h(x,v,r)$ with the public evidence term:
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{score}(v\mid x,r)=w^\top h(x,v,r)+\beta\frac{s(v,r)}{n(v,r)+\alpha}.
 \label{eq:score}
\end{equation}
For the queue instance, $r$ is the priority-contract context, $h(x,B,r)$ is the fixed task--candidate representation for producer $B$, and $n,s,u$ are counts over the versioned events that passed the gate. A recipient-only repair increases $u$ or creates an explicit \unknown{} record; it cannot increase $s$ for $B$.

The representation $h$, updater, and constants are replaceable implementation conditions rather than the scientific object. The score is a contract-level construction, not a theorem or a claim that one backbone is optimal. The selector uses a pre-registered exploration floor, records $p_t$, and uses preview--assignment--commit so that writing a future assignment cannot resample the selected candidate.

The same protocol yields three observable predictions. First, responsibility safety is measured by false producer-attribution and \unknown{} rates under recipient-only interventions. Second, temporal validity is measured by read-cut and assignment-order invariants and by the absence of target-outcome fields at selection. Third, bounded online service is measured by per-event update latency, backlog, state bytes, and complete cost. These predictions map to the RQ2--RQ4 endpoints; they are not assumptions that the score automatically satisfies.

\subsection{What counts as innovation}
The claimed object is the closed loop in which situated judgment becomes attributable evidence, evidence changes a legal future assignment, and that assignment has an unseen quality--cost consequence. Existing representations, frozen encoders, RLS, online logistic regression, periodic refitting, caches, and prompt templates are implementation or comparison choices. The required tests are matched composition, leave-one-mechanism-out ablations, evidence-content mutation, and a same-information contextual controller. If these controls reproduce the complete result, the closed-loop mechanism has no identifiable advantage.

\section{Benchmark and Baselines}

The primary track, \textbf{ArtifactRole}, is a TeamBench-derived candidate benchmark. It contains producer artifacts, recipient dependencies, use/rework/rejection actions, responsibility checks, later assignments, downstream adoption, and complete cost. \section{Experimental Questions and Matrix}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Research questions and estimands}
\textbf{RQ1 (information value).} Does an eligible situated judgment predict an independent producer-contract or later-use result beyond raw acceptance and terminal-only feedback? The primary estimands are out-of-sample log loss, Brier score, AUC when defined, calibration, and incremental predictive value on an independent root.

\textbf{RQ2 (assignment causality).} Does public evidence change an assignment made before target execution, including when the future owner did not produce the source judgment? We measure assignment-change probability, propensity-weighted future quality, adoption, rework, and complete cost. Evidence from producer $B$ cannot be consumed as if it belonged to producer $C$.

\textbf{RQ3 (online behavior).} Does the update path satisfy the service contract under selected-only delayed feedback? We report publish, read/assignment, and update p50/p95 latency, service lag, backlog, state bytes, CPU/GPU/RAM, token/tool/API calls, and replay time.

\textbf{RQ4 (stability and boundaries).} How does the method behave under task or peer drift, delayed or out-of-order feedback, version replacement, recipient-only work, mixed ownership, scorer failure, and resource failure? We report old-root peak and mean forgetting, drift recovery window, \unknown{} rate, correction consistency, and false producer attribution.

The primary unit of uncertainty is an independently reset stream. Episodes inside one stream are dependent. We use paired stream-level estimates, bootstrap or randomization intervals over streams, and pre-registered multiplicity control for the primary contrasts. Missing, invalid, or unknown events remain in the denominator and are never silently recoded as failures or successes.

\paragraph{Primary endpoint design.} The design reserves four card identifiers: \texttt{H1} uses independent-target Brier score (lower is better) for incremental situated signal; \texttt{H2} uses future assignment quality--complete-cost utility (higher is better) against the same-information control; \texttt{H3} uses selected-feedback update p95 latency and backlog under the fixed arrival budget (lower is better); and \texttt{H4} uses false producer-attribution rate under ownership mutations (lower is better). Each card is required to fix its control, minimum practically important difference or precision target, stream-level 95\% interval, and stopping rule before outcomes are observed; the current design-only manifest records which fields remain unfrozen.

Only these H1--H4 endpoints are primary for the headline interpretation. The remaining measures in the matrix are secondary or exploratory and cannot be promoted after outcomes are observed.

\subsection{Budget, statistics, and reporting}
Every model request reserves attempted calls, prompt tokens, maximum completion tokens, tool time, and storage before dispatch; errors release only unused reservations and retain their observed cost. We charge producer execution, recipient integration, judgment service, communication, retries, replay, verification, and returned artifacts. Development/search cost is reported separately from deployment cost. A pooled controller receives the same total capacity, and arms that do not use a judgment still pay the declared comparison cost or use a shared judgment call whose output is masked.

For each stream we record configuration, model route and version metadata, code and data hashes, seed, hardware, timestamps, raw API responses, token usage, intermediate actions, failure traces, \unknown{} reasons, and per-sample outcomes. We report means and medians with 95\% stream-level intervals, effect sizes for pre-registered contrasts, per-root heterogeneity, ITT and per-protocol denominators, and quality--cost Pareto points. The main endpoint is assignment-level future quality--complete-cost utility; source producer score is auxiliary and cannot replace it.

For every primary contrast, the experiment card fixes the direction, minimum practically important difference or precision target, stream-level 95\% interval, and stopping rule before confirmation. A row without those fields is a design specification rather than an interpretable result.

\subsection{Ablations and falsification}
The minimum ablation grid is: (A) no evidence/no update; (B) public evidence only, with persistent state unchanged; (C) delayed update only, without new public evidence; and (D) public evidence plus delayed update. Additional interventions remove responsibility filtering, selected-only restriction, delay, locality, correction/replay, or the assignment binding one at a time while holding information, model, candidate menu, and budget fixed. A content mutation that changes a visible judgment while holding terminal quality fixed must change the comparator score if the comparator actually consumes that judgment.

The main claim is withdrawn if an evidence publication implicitly changes persistent policy; if a recipient repair is credited to a producer; if assignment or read-cut order is invalid; if unknown or duplicate events update state; if contextual trust explains the complete effect; or if gains disappear at matched complete cost. A null, a trade-off, or a qualified protocol result is scientifically valid, but it cannot be written as successful role formation.

\paragraph{Phase rule.} Before selection, the policy sees only the candidate menu, public-row watermark, read-cut digest, and sealed propensity; hidden gold, unexecuted-candidate outcomes, and future target outcomes remain unavailable. During responsibility assessment, the record contains the judgment, action, owner, changed paths, ownership gate, and explicit \unknown{} reason. After the assignment seal, only the matching target quality/cost event can produce one selected-only update; a late or duplicate event without correction lineage is not a new label.

\section{Current Evidence and Expected Interpretation}
\label{sec:current}

Current real traces qualify engineering boundaries only. Two limited real-API chains logged source selection, recipient judgment, consumer execution, source snapshots, and a later assignment. Both consumers changed their own integration file, while the producer priority artifacts failed a separate import/constructor audit. The original four-check consumer score therefore did not cover the producer contract. The feedback mapping equaled its prior, so selection probabilities did not change. These observations motivated the responsibility gate, the separate producer audit, and the evidence-content mutation test.

The traces do not establish role specialization, predictive judgment, future quality improvement, or real-time training. The agents in those chains shared a fresh model and prompt without persistent individual experience, so identity labels cannot be interpreted as learned expertise. The benchmark and baseline matrix remain candidates until a second structural root, independent live histories, complete costs, and fair adapters qualify. No result cell in this manuscript is filled from those engineering receipts.

When confirmation evidence arrives, the interpretation follows a fixed truth table. Information gain without assignment or quality gain supports a predictive-signal claim only. Assignment gain without independent information leaves causal attribution unresolved. Quality gain with excess cost or unacceptable \unknown{} rate is a trade-off, not a quality--cost win. A result at insufficient root or stream coverage remains exploratory. This prevents a posterior change or a single successful trajectory from being promoted to the paper's central claim.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}

The mechanism is intentionally narrower than a claim that agents acquire general skills or that a workflow autonomously discovers experts. It studies responsibility opportunities in tasks with a real producer--recipient dependency. PeerSelect can isolate local online selection and drift, but it cannot replace ArtifactRole evidence. Conversely, an ArtifactRole result cannot be collapsed into a generic bandit payoff.

The main threats are judge error, selective exposure, root dependence, incomplete responsibility coverage, and cost inflation from recipient observation. The design measures judge reliability and selection propensity, preserves \unknown{} records, and uses independent roots and streams. It cannot infer a universal role semantics from a single workflow family. If the public evidence is too sparse, the correct outcome may be a well-calibrated null or a narrower task-conditioned result.

The final backbone and updater remain open. They will be chosen only after the qualified tracks and strong same-information baselines expose a bottleneck. A frozen representation with a small incremental head, RLS or online logistic regression, periodic refitting, and a bounded candidate updater are comparison conditions. A large GPU run is justified only when a real update or representation bottleneck is measured and the experiment card fixes the challenger, budget, and confirmation split.

\section{Conclusion}

This paper defines a precise object for self-evolving multi-agent roles: a recipient's situated judgment becomes responsibility-safe evidence, that evidence changes a future assignment before execution, and the later assignment is evaluated on unseen quality and complete cost. The protocol makes each arrow auditable and gives close alternatives the same information and budget. The current draft records the method contract, benchmark and baseline matrix, statistical plan, and falsification boundary while leaving empirical cells empty. The decisive result will be whether the complete loop improves future responsibility allocation beyond equally informed alternatives without sacrificing attribution safety, online service, stability, or cost.
