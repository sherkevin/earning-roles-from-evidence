\title[Learning Roles from Situated Peer Judgments]{Learning Roles from Situated Peer Judgments in Multi-Agent Collaboration}
\ccsdesc[500]{Computing methodologies~Multi-agent systems}
\keywords{Multiagent coordination, role learning, peer judgment, online adaptation}

\begin{abstract}
Multi-agent collaboration requires decisions about who should do what, yet a
team's final outcome does not identify which contributions helped the agents
that depended on them. We study whether judgments made by actual consumers of
delivered work can support role formation across tasks among agents without
assigned expert roles. We define a role as a context-dependent pattern of
responsibilities supported by an agent's collaboration history. The proposed
approach links consumer acceptance, actual use, and repair effort to the
producing agent, with later attributable outcomes correcting mistaken
judgments. Subsequent collaborators would use this evidence before assigning
new work, including collaborators who did not directly evaluate the producer.
This makes transfer across collaborators part of the hypothesis. A small
decision model updated from delayed feedback on selected peers is a candidate
implementation; its backbone and update rule remain to be determined. The
planned evaluation uses tasks with genuine dependencies, independent outcome
checks, and matched observation and computation budgets. Comparisons with
contextual trust, bandit selection, and peer-profile adaptation would test
whether situated judgments improve future team quality and total cost.
Removing judgments, shuffling their attribution, and withholding their transfer
would test the proposed mechanism. The central question is when collaboration
history supports useful divisions of responsibility beyond a private preference
for the best peer.
\end{abstract}

This document fixes a working question and prospective tests. It does not

\section{The Contribution We Need to Establish}

The target is a mechanism that uses situated judgments to change useful future
responsibilities. It must specify who may observe and share an evaluation,
what can be updated after delayed evidence arrives, and how the resulting
state changes a decision before work begins. A role label by itself is not an
outcome. The intended evidence is a change in actual assignments that improves
subsequent quality and complete collaboration cost.

The first development task separates a producer's code delivery from a
recipient's dependent integration work. These are temporary responsibilities,
not preset expert identities. A short, single-handoff probe can establish an
executable information boundary; it cannot establish decentralized role
formation or the full recursive collaboration setting.

\section{Prospective Discriminating Tests}

\paragraph{Task and signal qualification.}
Use real delivered work and record the recipient's initial judgment, actual
use, revisions, and completion cost. Keep independent evaluation outside the
agent observation boundary. Distinguish the quality of a delivery from the
quality obtained after another agent repairs it. Split by task structure
rather than treating renamed instances as independent problems.

\paragraph{Future responsibility.}
Evaluate legal pre-execution assignments on unseen tasks, including decisions
by collaborators who did not directly judge the candidate. Match initial
agents, observations, exploration opportunities, memory, and total resources.
Give the contextual trust or bandit comparator the same evidence. Test whether
task-dependent divisions of responsibility explain any benefit beyond selecting
one globally stronger peer.

\paragraph{Mechanism and cost.}
Remove judgment information, scramble its source within comparable contexts,
or withhold its transfer while preserving the remaining execution rules. If
real-time training becomes the contribution, compare one explicit updater with
online SGD, RLS, and periodic refitting. Measure old-task retention, adaptation,
and feedback-to-serving latency jointly; inexpensive parameter updates alone
do not establish useful real-time learning.

\section{Current Evidence Boundary}

The existing executable fixtures test protocol ordering and artifact lineage.
Their judgments and repairs are programmed, so they do not support learning
efficacy. Earlier API probes establish narrower transport and interaction
observations. There is no completed controlled result for the method proposed
here. All result claims and a final algorithm description must be supplied from
auditable experiments before this proposal can become a submission manuscript.
