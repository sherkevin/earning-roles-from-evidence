Discovered with the agent-reach Exa search route and checked against the official arXiv manuscript; this is a targeted comparison, not an exhaustive priority claim.

Sections 4.3–4.4 describe decentralized intent subscriptions and a local watchdog. The latter estimates first-hand publication usefulness/misbehavior, witness credibility and an overall peer reputation with discounted Beta-Bernoulli records. On routing a publication, an agent can solicit third-party witness posteriors, reject incompatible reports and combine admissible indirect evidence for reputation-aware brokerage. Section 5.5 reports that reactive subscriptions specialize a naive, generic agent pool. Thus **sparse decentralized communication, initially generic agents adapting roles, direct peer evaluation, second-hand testimony and witness reliability are all prior art**. Their conjunction cannot be presented as our innovation without a narrower identified operator and head-to-head evidence.

The inspected first-hand signal in §4.4 is a binary local watchdog judgment of whether a received publication is useful/consistent or misbehaving. In the examined paper text, it is not a sealed pair of **actual recipient pre-action acceptance plus later use/repair** linked to claim-specific public sources and actor-specific fault attribution across a dependent tool workflow. That is a candidate distinction to test, not an absence proof over all RAPS code or follow-up work. RAPS is a required strong conceptual control for any local witness-propagation claim; Meta-Team remains the close control for downstream teammate-profile evolution. The next experiment must give both methods comparable observations, communications and costs before claiming a difference.
