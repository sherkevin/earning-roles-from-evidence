# Native collaboration benchmark audit for peer-judged role formation

Status: primary papers, public repositories, and existing local AppWorld evidence inspected on 2026-09-23. **No benchmark or model API experiment was run for this audit.** Exact source URLs, pinned repository commits, locally cached PDF hashes, copied-file hashes, and licenses are in [sources.json](sources.json). The copied files are reference material only, outside the experimental runtime.

## Test the original question, not a substitute

The necessary phenomenon is a producer's delivered artifact changing what a recipient can do, how much work the recipient must repeat, or the result of its decision. The recipient's own acceptance, use, rejection, and repair can then be a noisy observation about that producer's capability for a *future* responsibility. To test learned roles, multiple persistent agent identities must be eligible for responsibilities, and later assignments must be allowed to change from evidence. Agent abilities and workflows may evolve under common rules. Merely having two prewritten roles, or a task with a read step followed by a write step, does not establish this chain.

## Why AppWorld remains useful, and what it cannot yet show

The local [18-task train manifest](../../../artifacts/analysis/aamas2027/peer_judgment_train_dev_candidates_20260923.json) has six Venmo-related scenario families with three closely related siblings each. Reading notes, contacts, receipts, or transaction history before writing to Venmo is a real task dependency. It does **not** imply that two agents must depend on one another: AppWorld's [official setup](https://github.com/StonyBrookNLP/appworld) assigns one supervisor instruction and app state to one autonomous agent, with state-based offline tests. The producer/consumer permissions and handoff are our adaptation. These train examples are development material, not 18 independent families or untouched confirmation cases; the research side has already inspected some task scorers.

In the actual [v4→v5 continuation](../../../artifacts/experiments/aamas2027/peer_judgment_resume_v5_20260923/report.md) on `3c13f5a_3`, the recipient independently read the bill, contacts, and Venmo users, sealed acceptance and intent to use, and made requests matching all three proposed rows. The official result failed solely on the recipient's final answer field after five other assertions passed. This is a valid *observed judgment and matching action*, not a causal demonstration that the proposal enabled or improved the action. The producer proposal also omitted the observation step that contained the bill and contact facts from its row-level citations. The evaluator ran offline and cannot be fed back into the actors during this case.

A small same-state **recipient-only control** with the same legal tools and full cost accounting is needed to see whether handing over a proposal changes outcome, elapsed work, API/LLM calls, or repairs. Then compare different eligible producer identities and proposal quality under the same recipient visibility and budget. If stronger resource or information separation is added to force a handoff, identify it as an AppWorld *adaptation* and keep that access rule identical across comparison arms. Do not claim natural multi-agent dependence from the original benchmark.

## What the two public alternatives actually offer

[Collab-Overcooked's paper](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.249/) describes 30 simulated cooking tasks across six complexity levels. Same-level tasks largely share workflows but change ingredients. Its public [assistant](collab_assistant_skill.txt) and [chef](collab_chef_skill.txt) prompts expose the fixed asymmetric action spaces: the assistant can cut or stir, while the chef can cook, bake, and deliver. The copied [boiled-egg recipe](collab_boiled_egg_recipe.txt) is a tiny task-input example; the [reference trajectory](collab_boiled_egg_reference.txt) is **evaluation-only gold** and must never enter a policy prompt. The [repository](https://github.com/YusaeMeow/Collab-Overcooked) documents Python 3.8, `mpi4py`, stepwise model calls, and evaluation scripts. Its small number of structural task families and fixed ability seats limit longitudinal role-learning claims even if one adds more persistent candidate identities. It is an open simulator, not real-world human interaction data.

[MultiAgentBench's paper](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.421/) introduces the MARBLE multi-agent framework and coding tasks adapted/generated from SRDD. The inspected [coding config](marble_coding_config_1.yaml) explicitly assigns three agent profiles and action prohibitions, including the intended create→revise→revise sequence. It also sets a five-iteration environment and an LLM evaluator. Those design choices are useful as an example of a controlled code-artifact handoff, but they would confound a claim that a role was earned through another agent's judgment. The public [repository](https://github.com/ulab-uiuc/MARBLE) requires Poetry/provider configuration and includes about 100 coding task configs; reworking profiles, assignment, and validation is material new engineering. Its coding prompts are generated/adapted benchmark cases, not unmodified human software projects.

Both repositories display MIT licenses; the exact notices are preserved as [Collab license](collab_LICENSE) and [MARBLE license](marble_LICENSE). The copied prompts/config are directly reusable for protocol and baseline design only. Do not silently import their fixed roles, gold trajectories, or LLM grader into our experimental runtime.

## Recommendation and leakage boundary

Continue AppWorld as the installed, objectively scored **development feasibility** environment. The next evidence gate is whether a genuine recipient-only or alternate-producer comparison shows an incremental handoff effect beyond final-action agreement. CooperBench supplies a more natural code-integration substrate, but its initial assignments, solo fallback and operational cost require a new protocol and explicit contribution checks. Collab-Overcooked is a useful *separate* natural-dependency stress test if that gate is met. MARBLE coding is lower priority because its concrete profiles preordain the roles and its inspected score path is not established as independent executable ground truth. None of these four sources is a drop-in test of the whole original question. If the project needs simultaneous necessary collaboration, exchangeable identities, peer judgment, later responsibility changes, and objective grading, it must clearly disclose a task adaptation or build a small new benchmark protocol around published task data.

For every future experiment, keep public task inputs separate from private AppWorld evaluators, Collab reference action trajectories, and any MARBLE scoring prompts/labels. Existing AppWorld train/scorer exposure must remain marked development. A published benchmark is a real dataset artifact, but AppWorld's people, the cooking world, and the coding prompts are simulated or generated. Real empirical evidence still requires actual LLM API calls, raw per-step logs, and preserved failures; this source audit produces no such result.
